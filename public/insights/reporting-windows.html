When the reporting windows open

Published 23 August 2026 · Reference

Most of a US college application is a set of boxes with fixed sizes and fixed dates. This page describes what those boxes ask for and when each window opens. It does not recommend a strategy.

What does the Common App ask for?

For the 2026–27 cycle, the section formerly called Activities was renamed Activities and Experiences. It holds ten entries. Each entry has three text fields with hard character limits, and spaces and punctuation count against them.

FieldLimitNotes
Position or leadership title50 charactersOptional as of this cycle
Organization name100 characters—
Description of involvement150 charactersRoughly a sentence and a half

Alongside the text, each entry carries structured data: the activity type, the grade levels of participation, whether it ran during the school year or over breaks, hours per week, weeks per year, and whether the student intends to continue in college.

A separate Honors section holds five entries at 100 characters each. The distinction the application draws is between the recognition and the involvement that produced it: a state championship is an honor, and the team that competed for it is an activity.

Which grades count?

The participation checkboxes on each activity entry are grades 9, 10, 11, 12, and post-graduate. There is no box for grade 8 or earlier.

That has a consequence worth stating plainly. Work done in middle school is not reportable in that section on its own terms. What it can do is establish the start of something that continues into grade 9 — a sport, an instrument, a research interest, a business — where the ninth-grade-onward portion is what gets checked.

The checkboxes are also a factual claim. Checking four years for an activity that began in eleventh grade is the kind of statement a counselor recommendation or a transcript can contradict.

When can a Division I coach first make contact?

NCAA recruiting rules set the earliest date a college coach may initiate contact, and the date varies by sport and division. For most Division I sports, including swimming and diving, it is 15 June following the athlete’s sophomore year. From that date, coaches may call, text, email, message on social media, and extend verbal offers.

DateWhat opens (D1 swimming and diving)
Any timeCamp brochures, questionnaires, non-athletic publications, NCAA educational material
15 June after sophomore yearCoach-initiated calls, texts, email and social messages; verbal offers
1 August before junior yearOfficial and unofficial campus visits; off-campus contact

Two asymmetries sit inside those dates. The restrictions bind coaches, not families — a prospective athlete may write to a program at any age, though a Division I coach cannot respond substantively before the contact date. And the rules loosen by division: Division II coaches may communicate considerably earlier, and Division III operates with almost no timing restriction at all.

The practical shape of this is that a coach’s recruiting list is assembled from public results well before the date on which the coach is permitted to say so.

What does the NCAA Eligibility Center require?

Athletic eligibility is a separate certification from admission. Division I requires sixteen core courses, a minimum grade-point average in those courses, high school graduation, and an amateurism certification. Division II also requires sixteen core courses. Because the requirement is expressed in specific courses rather than an overall transcript, it constrains which classes are taken in which years, and it begins binding in grade 9.

Why the gap matters

Set the two calendars beside each other and a gap appears. Things happen across roughly a decade — a first competitive time, a course that turned out to be the start of a subject, a job, a role nobody wrote down. They are reported inside a few weeks in the autumn of grade 12, into ten boxes of 150 characters, plus five honors, plus a set of grade-level checkboxes that have to be accurate.

Nothing in the application asks for a decade of evidence. What it asks for is a small number of specific, checkable claims. The difficulty families describe is not usually deciding what to write — it is reconstructing, years later, the dates, hours per week, weeks per year, official titles and exact organization names that the structured fields require, from memory and a folder of photographs.
